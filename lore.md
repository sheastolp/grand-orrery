# Verrowen lore

This file is the single source of truth the narrator reads. Edit it as the universe grows.

UNIVERSE: The Grand Orrery. It's the artificers' name for the universe, picturing its worlds as moving parts in one great mechanism. Only high-ranking and learned people know the name. Worlds connect through rifts: random tears that open and close, pulling in people, monsters or whole buildings, which is why the same monsters appear on different worlds. Some artificers can open a rift on purpose, using a doorway as the boundaries for the magic.
WORLD: Verrowen. This is a scholarly name found in books. Villages own few books, and only kingdoms have libraries, so common folk just say "the world."
TONE: Medieval fantasy with D&D-style monsters.
MAGIC: Common but weak. It assists work, it doesn't replace it. Types: elemental (wind, fire, earth), strength enhancement, detection. Everyday uses include mending torn or cracked things and loosening or firming soil. Healing specialists are very rare and only ever human, from humans' affinity for it; a small or medium village may have 2 or 3 at a time (Stoutwell has 2). Healers use magic to assist the body's own healing. Artificers aren't healers but can still heal, leaning on natural healing and on drawing other people's healing magic, sometimes nefariously. A fire user who can boil a pot of water counts as super skilled. Everyone has a well of mana. It refills more slowly than the body heals, and there's no visible bar. Running out (mana deprivation) causes sickness, dizziness or passing out. Elves are most magical, then humans, then halflings. People can have latent magic they don't know about. Objects hold mana but can't make it. A charged item normally works only for the person whose mana is in it.
ARTIFICERS: Extremely rare. They understand all magic, store other people's mana in objects, never lend their own strength, and can sense latent magic but not awaken it. A secret artificer item, the Seeker's Lodestone (a round, flat disc of mirrorstone, a rare black glass), can locate other artificers.
ITEMS (Murrow's shop, Monstradius): Hearthstones (fire-charged river stones, keep a stove warm one night per charge, still need embers; rare, pricey). Featherhooks (Doric's iron hooks with a wind pattern; halve a dragged log's weight using the holder's latent wind, better for humans; too long leaves you winded). Wardbells (on the walls; ring when something big leaves the Monster Wood; big vs small only; refreshed by a detection user every few weeks). Truthglass (quartz lens; says real or fake for coins, seals, papers, nothing more). Rift compass (secret, ~80% done, points where worlds are thin; even Murrow doesn't know how it works).
STOUTWELL: A 600-year-old walled village of about 220 people: about 72% human, 28% halfling, plus the Wellhouse family, its only elves, who keep to their tavern and are ostracized. It's known for its stout ale and ornate smithing. Its central well dried up 550 years ago because of the blight; the artificer Murrow recently built irrigation from the river. Gates face north, east, south and west. The west gate opens onto the croplands, the Woodland Road and the Monster Wood. Districts: NW is middle- to upper-class housing; NE is Murrow's shop Monstradius and his research building; SW is the shopping district with the Wellhouse tavern; the slums lie along the south and SE, with their own poorer housing and Doric's smithy. Near the center are the dried well and the Gathering Hall, where the Elderhold meets. Grain and lumber mills sit on the river outside the east wall; the lumber yard is inside by the east gate. South of the village lies the Ashen Reach, a plain of grey soil where nothing grows tall; it's Stoutwell's training grounds, right beside the slums along the south wall.
LIFE: Children are schooled together, then the Elders place them into groups (Village Workers, Herders, Farmers, Gatherers, the Guard, the Hearthcircle). People may switch jobs within reason. Homes burn firewood. Gatherers find dead trees with detection magic and drag them out with wind magic, under Guard escort.
DEFENSE AND LAW: The Guard is stout halfling shield-bearers plus human archers on the walls. Villagers rotate a couple of days a month. The halfling Guard is small and overworked. Punishments range from stocks and flogging to exile and execution. Weregild (payment in place of punishment) is accepted.
LEADERSHIP: The Hearthcircle is the ruling group, placed by talent, never by birth. The Elderhold is five halfling Elders who vote (Wendeline Brackenford, Fenwick Larkspur, Bertram Quillby, Ysolde Ambergate, Clementine Rook) plus Osric, their non-voting spokesperson and Leader. Bertram and Ysolde are secretly corrupt. Osric suspects but can't prove it, and he hides a surname tied to Kingdom nobility.
PEOPLE: Tobin Ashby (halfling, 33, blacksmith's apprentice; determined, loyal, efficient). Doric (halfling, 67, strict, proud master smith; stung that the Hearthcircle buys its fine metalwork from the capital, not him). Murrow (artificer over 550 years old, race unknown, hidden under a deep hood; cold and businesslike, never mean; fears the Kingdom). Hesketh Reeve (human, 48, Guard Captain; harsh but fair). Elders: Wendeline (149, eldest, keeper of customs), Fenwick (145, ex-Guard, blunt), Bertram (138, keeps the ledgers and tithe counts), Ysolde (133, harvest stores and rationing, charming), Clementine (131, newest, curious about magic, deals with Murrow).
BEYOND: The Monster Wood (goblins, plus trolls and large beasts native to its deep woods) lies to the west. An old blight is spreading through its deep woods, driving desperate goblins to raid Stoutwell's crops. Past the wood is a corrupt Kingdom and its capital, where the gloomy rock gnome artificer Glumtom works for the Kingdom, unwillingly: he heals, builds and raises the dead to labor, but never fights. He is distant family to Murrow. GuildScribe is the Kingdom's adventurers' guild hall in the capital (none exists in Stoutwell), named for its guildscribes, the clerks who keep its records: a parchment for each adventurer (race, trade, deeds, rank), registered companies (parties), the Archives (a library of spells, items, rules and monster lore), a bounty board and bestiary paying in gold, silver and copper, and an arena for sanctioned duels. It serves the Kingdom first. Guild members are on the Kingdom's books and can be drafted, and a seasonal census puts everyone else in the draft pool too. A few adventurers hunt the Monster Wood from the west, but none have made it past the blighted goblin lands, so none have reached Stoutwell. The bestiary has no entry yet for the large beasts of the deep woods. Its senior guildscribe, the Scribe, is sour and unimpressed but secretly cares; anyone who tries to rob her is fined into the hall's swear jar. Threadbare peddlers (Tansy Ninefingers, Pruneface Yorik, Old Corrin, Widow Hesk and many more) take turns at an open stall in the capital's market and haggle hard. The Wandering Clerk, a guildscribe who broke from the hall, keeps a ledger of their own on the Kingdom's roads, swearing in adventurers where there's no guild; that road could bring the first guild ledger to Stoutwell. Some peddler relics may come from the Monster Wood. Merchants with escorts and a tax representative travel the Woodland Road. Somewhere far past the Kingdom stands a divination tower; little is known of it.
OTHER WORLDS: Arandur, an elf ranger and pickpocket from another world, was pulled out of it into another. In his youth, a rift dropped him in a village near Stoutwell (name not yet known). He was raised in a forest cabin by his hunter mother Silwen, who vanished with his distant father Faelan; he suspects a voiceless spectre that taught him to track is Silwen. Abandoned, he became a thief and a self-taught archer. He hides everything under contempt and silence, talks down magic, and fears trolls but can't fully hate them.
