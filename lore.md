# Verrowen lore

UNIVERSE: The Grand Orrery. It's the artificers' name for the universe, picturing its worlds as moving parts in one great mechanism. Only high-ranking and learned people know the name. Worlds connect through rifts: random tears that open and close, pulling in people, monsters or whole buildings, which is why the same monsters appear on different worlds. Some artificers can open a rift on purpose, using a doorway as the boundaries for the magic.
WORLD: Verrowen. This is a scholarly name found in books. Villages own few books, and only kingdoms have libraries, so common folk just say "the world."
TONE: Medieval fantasy with D&D-style monsters.
MAGIC: Common but weak. It assists work, it doesn't replace it. Types: elemental (wind, fire, earth), strength enhancement, detection. Everyday uses include mending torn or cracked things and loosening or firming soil. Healing specialists are very rare and only ever human, from humans' affinity for it; a small or medium village may have 2 or 3 at a time (Stoutwell has 2). Healers use magic to assist the body's own healing. Artificers aren't healers but can still heal, leaning on natural healing and on drawing other people's healing magic, sometimes nefariously. A fire user who can boil a pot of water counts as super skilled. Everyone has a well of mana. It refills more slowly than the body heals, and there's no visible bar. Running out (mana deprivation) causes sickness, dizziness or passing out. Fire is the opposite of cold, so a fire user low on mana grows cold, more so the emptier they are. Elves are most magical, then humans, then halflings. People can have latent magic they don't know about. Objects hold mana but can't make it. A charged item normally works only for the person whose mana is in it; a very rare ability, nearly as rare as artificing, lets an item work without its owner. Wind users are rare and far more often human. Strength enhancement is fairly common (Tobin uses it to hammer hot steel).
ARTIFICERS: Extremely rare. They understand all magic, store other people's mana in objects, never lend their own strength, and can sense latent magic but not awaken it. Murrow's Elemental Test: shown an array of items (a stone, a feather), an injured child he's healing is drawn to the one their secretly drawn mana reacts in, revealing their element; most people find their element on their own. A secret artificer item, the Seeker's Lodestone (a round, flat disc of mirrorstone, a rare black glass), can locate other artificers.
ITEMS (Murrow's shop, Monstradius): Hearthstones (fire-charged river stones, keep a stove warm one night per charge, still need embers; rare, pricey). Featherhooks (Doric's iron hooks with a wind pattern; halve a dragged log's weight using the holder's latent wind, better for humans; too long leaves you winded). Wardbells (on the walls; ring when something big leaves the Monster Wood; big vs small only; refreshed by a detection user every few weeks). Truthglass (quartz lens; says real or fake for coins, seals, papers, nothing more). Rift compass (secret, ~80% done, points where worlds are thin; even Murrow doesn't know how it works). The Rift compass doesn't point to a world: it shows how thick the rifts are nearby, and a needle twitching wildly means many unstable tears. It can't tell you which world a rift leads to.
RESOURCES: Hearthstones are charged river stones; the Truthglass is rare clear quartz; mirrorstone is rare black glass shaped by magic. Doric forges iron (Featherhook hooks to Murrow's specifications, the Guard's gear, ornate work) with a smith's hammer. Timber comes from dead-tree runs, is cut at the lumber mill and stored in the lumber yard. A few short, twisted ash trees grow on the Ashen Reach; their dense, tough wood is too short for beams but prized by the woodworkers for tool handles, weapon hilts and arrow shafts. Leather comes from the herders' cattle, worked at the tannery, and clothes everyone. The grain mill grinds flour. The fishing settlement's catch feeds Stoutwell. The halfling Guard fights behind big shields; human archers carry bows. Coin is gold, silver and copper; Murrow takes coin or barter on the same terms for everyone, and his rare items cost more than people expect.
STOUTWELL: A 600-year-old walled village of about 220 people: about 72% human, 28% halfling, plus the Wellhouse family, its only elves, who keep to their tavern and are ostracized. It's known for its stout ale and ornate smithing, and is named for its well, once the source of that ale. Its founding stories were never passed down. The ale never lost its potency, but once the well died and water had to be hauled from the river, the supply became very restricted; Murrow's irrigation might mean more ale for the first time in centuries (who controls it is undecided). The Wellhouse elves moved here centuries ago for their soft spot for sharing and caring, and do nothing for the village beyond the tavern. Its central well dried up 550 years ago because of the blight; the artificer Murrow recently built irrigation from the river. Gates face north, east, south and west. The west gate opens onto the croplands, the Woodland Road and the Monster Wood. Districts: NW is middle- to upper-class housing; NE is Murrow's shop Monstradius and his research building; SW is the shopping district with the Wellhouse tavern; the slums lie along the south and SE, with their own poorer housing and Doric's smithy. Near the center are the dried well and the Gathering Hall, where the Elderhold meets. Grain and lumber mills sit on the river outside the east wall; the lumber yard is inside by the east gate. South of the village lies the Ashen Reach, a plain of grey soil where nothing grows tall; it's Stoutwell's training grounds, right beside the slums along the south wall. The river flows south past the mills and opens into a lake southeast of the village. An impassable wall of mountains runs along the east and turns west along the south, past the Ashen Reach; the lake sits in its corner and no one goes over. On the lake's far shore from Stoutwell, at the foot of the mountains, is a small fishing settlement (name undecided) of usually 5 to 10 people. They fish and are a source of food for Stoutwell but aren't part of the village. SECRET: they guard and hide a passage through the mountains to an unknown place on the other side; outsiders don't know of it, and what lies beyond is undecided. The slums are there for two reasons: the ash dust and the noise of the drills make the south wall the least wanted land in the village, so it's where poorer families could afford to live; and the slums supply most of the Guard's recruits, so trainees live a short walk from the grounds.
RACES: Halflings are stout, thick-skinned and naturally heat-resistant (well suited to smithing), quick, quiet and lucky, and homebodies who love good food and a warm hearth. They reach adulthood around 20, live to about 150 and have children less often, so the oldest villagers are almost always halflings and the shortest in any crowd. Humans rarely pass 100; they're leaner, more attuned to elemental magic, and do about 70% of the wood chopping.
LIFE: Children are schooled together, then the Elders place them into groups (Village Workers, Herders, Farmers, Gatherers, the Guard, the Hearthcircle). People may switch jobs within reason. Heavy jobs like smithing lean male, but nothing is restricted by gender. Village Workers stay inside the walls: smiths, the tannery, woodworkers (weapon hilts, ornate structures). Herders mainly raise cattle; the Elders keep a couple of cows just to look at. Farmers work the croplands, which take up the most space. Gatherers also forage at the forest edge, and the human gatherers gather the stout ale's ingredients. The village is communal and peaceful, with little internal trouble. Homes burn firewood. Gatherers find dead trees with detection magic and drag them out with wind magic, under Guard escort.
DEFENSE AND LAW: The Guard is stout halfling shield-bearers plus human archers on the walls. Villagers rotate a couple of days a month between four posts (inside the village, the croplands, the walls, and escorting gathering groups), around a full-time Guard of men and women picked for strength. The guard tithe pays for it: each household gives a share of its harvest, crafts or labor, and wealthier families like the millers give more. The halfling Guard is small and overworked, which shows as a little skimming and shift dodging; the extreme punishments keep defiance minimal. Hesketh dislikes weregild but accepts it because it's the law. Punishments range from stocks and flogging to exile and execution. Weregild (payment in place of punishment) is accepted.
LEADERSHIP: The Hearthcircle is the ruling group, placed by talent, never by birth. The Elderhold is five halfling Elders who vote (Wendeline Brackenford, Fenwick Larkspur, Bertram Quillby, Ysolde Ambergate, Clementine Rook) plus Osric, their non-voting spokesperson and Leader. Bertram and Ysolde are secretly corrupt. Bertram's ledgers make tithe skimming vanish on paper, Ysolde steers bigger harvest shares to favored crop workers, and together they steer favorites into easier jobs. Osric suspects but can't prove it, and he hides a surname tied to someone high in the Kingdom. Osric (halfling, 142) rose from near-peasant through the Guard and the Hearthcircle; warm in public, very calculating underneath. He and Hesketh came up through the Guard in the same years on opposite shifts. A Hearthcircle member rises to the Elderhold only when a seat opens, by vote. The Hearthcircle is richer, trades with the merchants most and buys its fine metalwork from the capital.
PEOPLE: Tobin Ashby (halfling, 33, blacksmith's apprentice; determined, loyal, efficient). Doric (halfling, 67, strict, proud master smith; stung that the Hearthcircle buys its fine metalwork from the capital, not him). Murrow (artificer over 550 years old, race unknown, hidden under a deep hood; cold and businesslike, never mean; fears the Kingdom; his shop Monstradius is his workshop, and his ordinary-looking home in the NW housing district, three blocks away, is known to no one; he can cast a time-limited Nondetection-style spell and is researching an item that lasts; he was likely around when the well died and won't speak of it; Doric is an acquaintance; he has a soft spot for one kind of person, still undecided). Hesketh Reeve (human, 48, Guard Captain, picked from the ranks for his prowess; harsh but fair, not corrupt; a human over a mostly halfling Guard is part of the village's built-in bias). Elders: Wendeline (149, eldest, keeper of customs), Fenwick (145, ex-Guard, blunt, respects Hesketh), Bertram (138, keeps the ledgers and tithe counts), Ysolde (133, harvest stores and rationing, charming), Clementine (131, newest, curious about magic, deals with Murrow). Burn (human, about 45, short, big mustache, red cap; the Wellhouse's longtime regular, named for the one-line roasts he drops from his bar stool, only when something's actually funny, always about what someone said or did, never who they are; the Wellhouse elves' one friend; swears red-capped forest mushrooms make you grow; a little fire magic, enough to light a pipe; came down the Woodland Road with the merchants about 15 years ago and nobody knows his real name; SECRET: a court jester from the Kingdom's capital who fled after a joke landed on the wrong noble).
BEYOND: The Monster Wood (goblins, plus trolls and large beasts native to its deep woods; tall monsters and floating ones shaped the walls and the archers, and true flyers are rare) lies to the west, filling the land between the Kingdom and the croplands and running south into the mountains. An old blight is spreading through its deep woods, driving desperate goblins to raid Stoutwell's crops. Past the wood is a corrupt Kingdom and its capital, where the gloomy rock gnome artificer Glumtom works for the Kingdom, unwillingly: he heals, builds and raises the dead to labor, but never fights. He is distant family to Murrow. A tinkerer since childhood who ran a repair workshop, he was drafted as a registered Kingdom citizen and works only for them, in his cluttered workshop in the capital, for high pay that funds his reanimation research. He heals their guards, builds their tools and weapons, labors, and raises a few undead to build walls and buildings. He comes across as gloomy and keeps to himself. He made the Seeker's Lodestone, which the Kingdom doesn't know exists; he could find Murrow with it but isn't looking, and Murrow doesn't know of it. GuildScribe is the Kingdom's adventurers' guild hall in the capital (none exists in Stoutwell), named for its guildscribes, the clerks who keep its records: a parchment for each adventurer (race, trade, deeds, rank), registered companies (parties), the Archives (a library of spells, items, rules and monster lore), a bounty board and bestiary paying in gold, silver and copper, and an arena for sanctioned duels. It serves the Kingdom first. Guild members are on the Kingdom's books and can be drafted, and a seasonal census puts everyone else in the draft pool too. A few adventurers hunt the Monster Wood from the west, but none have made it past the blighted goblin lands, so none have reached Stoutwell. The deep woods' large beasts are hollowstags (stags nearly fifteen feet tall whose hollow antlers whistle as they run), rootwyrms (serpents as thick as a mill wheel, forced up as the Blight kills the roots they live in) and the ancient Mosswarden, so old a small forest grows on its back. The Blight is pushing them, thornback boars and floating driftmaws west toward the Kingdom, and gloamowls, dog-sized dusk owls, are among the wood's few true flyers. GuildScribe has posted bounties, but no one has yet brought back proof of the deep woods' beasts. Its senior guildscribe, the Scribe, is sour and unimpressed but secretly cares; anyone who tries to rob her is fined into the hall's swear jar. Threadbare peddlers (Tansy Ninefingers, Pruneface Yorik, Old Corrin, Widow Hesk and many more) take turns at an open stall in the capital's market and haggle hard. The Wandering Clerk, a guildscribe who broke from the hall, keeps a ledger of their own on the Kingdom's roads, swearing in adventurers where there's no guild; that road could bring the first guild ledger to Stoutwell. The Clerk and some of the peddlers (Wobble, Pruneface Yorik, Tansy Ninefingers, Old Corrin, Tam Pockets, Squint, Nan Gullyfoot, Kettle, Sella Windrags) follow the Clerk's road out to the Kingdom's edge and sometimes down the Woodland Road with the merchants as far as Stoutwell; the rest live in the capital. None of GuildScribe's people live in Stoutwell. GuildScribe's registered adventurers, ranked Novice, Journeyman, Seasoned or Veteran by their parchments, are a wider mix of peoples than Stoutwell sees (dwarves, drow, half-orcs, dragonborn, tieflings). Veterans include Sun (high elf monk), MuzzledKitty (tiefling wizard) and Blackass (dragonborn druid) of the company Science Club; Xan (tiefling druid) of Nuthaus; nobody (mountain dwarf barbarian) and Momma_Enya (dragonborn warlock) of No Trolls Here; Fel (drow fighter) of the Dweebs; Stabitha (human rogue) and nogood59 (gnome cleric). Others include Shadowflame, Briarpike, Mistkettle, Grimknot, Quack, Ryos, Moonkettle, Cinderfoot, Conanski, Ironmantle, Bramthistle, Mistcrest, Hawkthistle, Thunderwing and Bramblehelm. They hunt bounties across the Kingdom; none has reached Stoutwell. Some peddler relics may come from the Monster Wood. Merchants with escorts and a tax representative travel the Woodland Road, which is relatively safe but still dangerous. The tax representative is chosen for minimal bias, though the Kingdom's corruption can still creep in. Somewhere far past the Kingdom stands a divination tower; little is known of it.
KINGDOM: The Kingdom runs on paper. Its rolls (census, tax, the draft list, the register of artificers, GuildScribe's parchments) are kept in the Rolls House beside the Crown's seat in the capital, and its corruption is mostly about whose names get written, lost or moved for a bribe. The monarch is a distant banner; a handful of great houses hold the offices that matter (keeper of the rolls, master of the mint, warden of the roads, master of the draft) and pass them down like property. Osric's hidden surname may belong to one of them. House Harrowmere holds the Highwrit, the court office whose clerks copy, polish and seal every decree, writ and letter of the Crown and the Rolls House; its seal makes a document look right but never checks that it's true. Registered citizens get the Kingdom's law, its markets and bounty coin, and can be drafted for garrisons, labor or a rare skill. Every artificer the Kingdom finds goes into the register and is put to work, the way Glumtom was, which is exactly what Murrow fears. Enchanted items made in the Kingdom carry a registered artificer's mark; unmarked ones raise questions. Stoutwell has never been counted: the census stops at the Monster Wood, and the village pays its tax as one lump sum carried home by the tax representative, which is why no one there has been drafted and why a single guild ledger could end the deal. The capital is a walled city west of the Monster Wood, at the far end of the Woodland Road and the Clerk's road, ringed by the heartland's farms and river towns (grain, wool, timber; coin is minted in the capital). Inside are the Crown's seat, the Rolls House, the mint, the workshop quarter that makes the Hearthcircle's fine metalwork, GuildScribe's hall and arena, the market square and Glumtom's workshop; the poor quarters sit outside the old wall, where newer walls, some built by Glumtom's undead, are still going up. Towns and waystations grow poorer and less watched to the east; merchants hire escorts at the last waystation, where the Woodland Road leaves for Stoutwell. The Kingdom grew outward from the capital over centuries and stopped at the wood. Most of its people are human, with halflings and gnomes common in the towns; the capital draws everyone.
OTHER WORLDS: Arandur, an elf ranger and pickpocket from another world, was pulled out of it into another. In his youth, a rift dropped him in a village near Stoutwell (name not yet known). He was raised in a forest cabin by his hunter mother Silwen, who vanished with his distant father Faelan; he suspects a voiceless spectre that taught him to track is Silwen. Abandoned, he became a thief and a self-taught archer. He hides everything under contempt and silence, talks down magic, and fears trolls but can't fully hate them.
WELLHOUSE: The Wellhouse family are elves who moved here centuries ago for their soft spot for sharing and caring, and do nothing for the village beyond the tavern. They have a few children but don’t make many—they don’t want trouble. Their name is not yet known.
SEEKER'S LODESTONE: The round, flat disc of mirrorstone can locate other artificers. It does not work reliably, particularly if the target artificer is actively concealing their presence through concealment magic. The Lodestone requires a consistent stream of mana from the user for accurate readings, otherwise the needle will spin wildly, giving false readings within a thirty foot range. It responds best to artificers who are actively using their own mana signatures rather than passively holding them. The Seeker’s Lodestone has been broken into three pieces across the kingdom since its creation; they remain undiscovered.
MONSTER WOOD: The wood is filled with goblins, plus trolls and large beasts native to its deep woods; tall monsters and floating ones shaped the walls and the archers, and true flyers are rare. A blight is spreading through its deep woods, driving desperate goblins to raid Stoutwell's crops. GuildScribe has posted bounties, but no one has yet brought back proof of the deep woods’ beasts.  The rootwyrms have been driven out by the Blight and they aren't returning; their remains now feed giant fungi growing in the darkest parts of the forest.

These don’t actively detect monsters, registering unnatural shifts in energy correlating with their presence. Murrow and Burn maintain this system. Prolonged operation drains Murrow’s personal mana reserves, making him colder and weaker.

Abandoned, he became a thief and a self-taught archer. He hides everything under contempt and silence, talks down magic, and fears trolls but can't fully hate them.
